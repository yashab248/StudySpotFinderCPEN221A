# Project Name: Chupa

*A student-focused project to make campus study time easier, more productive, and less stressful.*

> **Project name and logo:** Chupa
## Our Mission

**No one should lose study time looking for a place to study.**

## Our Goal and Values

Our goal is to help students make better use of their time on campus by addressing the difficulties of finding a suitable place to study. We are starting with the University of British Columbia (UBC), but the problem is relevant to students at other universities as well.

The values guiding our team are:

- **Accessibility and inclusion:** Students have different study needs, schedules, and accessibility requirements. Finding a useful space should not depend on already knowing the campus well.
- **Time and well-being:** Time spent searching for a place to work is time taken away from studying, resting, and other responsibilities.
- **Reliable information:** Students should be able to make decisions based on information that is clear, relevant, and trustworthy.
- **Collaboration:** We value open communication, shared responsibility, and constructive feedback as we design and build our project.

## The Problem

At a large university like UBC, finding an appropriate place to study can be surprisingly difficult. Students often do not know which campus spaces are open, which are crowded, or which suit the kind of work they need to do. A quiet space for individual study may be unsuitable for a group project, while a space without accessible entrances, charging outlets, or other important amenities may not meet a student's needs. Operating hours can also change on weekends, statutory holidays, and during different times of the academic year, making it harder to plan ahead.

These uncertainties force students to spend valuable time walking between buildings or checking multiple sources, sometimes only to arrive at a full or closed study area. This is especially frustrating for students with short breaks between classes and commuters who cannot easily return home to study. Well-known spaces can attract most of the attention, while less familiar study areas may be overlooked.

Information about study spaces is spread across library pages, individual building information, and recommendations from other students. While these sources can be useful, they do not consistently communicate the current crowd level, study environment, amenities, and availability of a space in one place. As a result, students may lose time, experience unnecessary stress, and struggle to find environments where they can work effectively.

**Our central problem:** Students have difficulty knowing where they can study on campus, when those spaces are available, and whether they fit their needs.

## Project Scope

Our initial focus is the experience of students looking for study spaces at **UBC**. The broader problem is not unique to UBC, so we want our approach to remain relevant to other campuses as the project develops.
