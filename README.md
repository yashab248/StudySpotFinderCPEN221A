# CHUPA

**Campus Hub for Uncrowded Places & Availability**

*A project focused on helping UBC students spend less time searching for study spaces and more time studying.*

> **Logo:** To be selected or designed by the team.

## Our Mission

**No one should lose study time looking for a place to study.**

## Our Goal and Values

Our goal is to make campus life less stressful and more productive by addressing the difficulty of finding study spaces that suit students' needs. We are starting at the **University of British Columbia (UBC)**, while keeping in mind that students at other campuses face similar challenges.

Our values are:

- **Accessibility and inclusion:** A suitable study environment should be easier to find for students with different needs, schedules, and familiarity with campus.
- **Time and well-being:** Students' time matters, especially during busy academic periods and short breaks between classes.
- **Clarity and trust:** Information about spaces should be understandable and reliable; uncertainty should not be presented as fact.
- **Collaboration:** We value shared responsibility, honest feedback, and a supportive team environment.

## The Problem

At a large university like UBC, finding an appropriate place to study can be surprisingly difficult. Students often do not know which campus spaces are open, which are crowded, or which suit the kind of work they need to do. A quiet space for individual study may be unsuitable for a group project, while a space without accessible entrances, charging outlets, or other important amenities may not meet a student's needs. Operating hours can also change on weekends, statutory holidays, and during different times of the academic year, making it harder to plan ahead.

These uncertainties force students to spend valuable time walking between buildings or checking multiple sources, sometimes only to arrive at a full or closed study area. This is especially frustrating for students with short breaks between classes and commuters who cannot easily return home to study. Well-known spaces may become crowded while lesser-known study areas are overlooked.

Information about study spaces is spread across library pages, individual building information, and recommendations from other students. These sources can be useful, but they do not always provide a consistent picture of a space's hours, crowding, environment, and amenities. As a result, students can lose study time, experience unnecessary stress, and struggle to find a place where they can work effectively.

**Our central problem:** Students have difficulty knowing where they can study on campus, when those spaces are available, and whether they fit their needs.

## Scope and Project Requirements

CHUPA is a **UBC campus-life project** planned for **October 16 – December 4, 2026** (approximately seven weeks). Our five-person team has an expected commitment of about **3 hours per person per week**, or **15 person-hours per week** and approximately **105 person-hours overall**.

Given that timeframe, we will focus on a manageable first version rather than attempting to cover every study space or build every proposed feature. We will define an achievable scope during the design phase and prioritize the features most directly related to the problem.

The course expects our project to include:

- **User interaction:** Students can interact with the application to obtain useful information.
- **File-based data:** Preferably, a dataset that can be read from one or more files.
- **Client–server communication:** Interaction between a client and server running on different machines.
- **An external library:** At least one library beyond the standard Java libraries (or comparable standard library for the language we choose).

We will also apply the course's software engineering concepts, including **specifications, testing, abstract data types, and concurrency**, where relevant to our design. Our technology stack and detailed requirements have not yet been finalized.

## Development Process

Throughout the project, our team will:

- Break work into milestones and track progress using **GitHub Issues**.
- Develop changes on **Git branches** and merge reviewed work back into the shared codebase.
- Write and run tests to verify behavior against the project's specifications.
- Maintain clear build and run instructions so that the application is easy to compile and use.
- Record any meaningful use of generative AI, including prompts and how we independently checked its output, while being mindful of its environmental cost.
